Planning an Architectural Walkthrough Video: A Client Checklist

Duration, route, aspect ratio, music, branding and the key moments to show — what to decide before production begins.

By Muhammad Kamran · October 24, 2026 · 8 min read

A walkthrough video is one of the most persuasive ways to present a building — and one of the easiest to get wrong. Without a plan, it can become a long, wandering camera move that loses viewers in the first few seconds. With a plan, it becomes a short film that shows exactly what matters.

This checklist covers the decisions to make before production starts. Most of them take minutes to think through, and each one saves time later. For an overview of the service itself, see architectural walkthrough animation.

1. What should the video achieve?

Start with one sentence. For example:

  • "Show buyers the flow from the entrance to the living area and the main bedroom."
  • "Introduce the project's exterior and landscaping for a launch event."
  • "Help our family understand the planned house before we start building."

That sentence decides almost everything else: which spaces to show, how long the video should be and where it will be watched.

2. Walkthrough, flythrough or both?

  • An interior walkthrough moves through rooms at eye level, the way a visitor would.
  • An exterior flythrough approaches or orbits the building from above to show the facade, site and surroundings.

Many presentations open with a short exterior sequence and then move inside. If you only need to show one thing, choose one; a focused video is usually stronger.

3. Duration

Longer is not better. Every extra second needs modeling, rendering and editing — and viewers' attention.

  • Social media cuts are often well under a minute.
  • Presentation videos commonly run around one to two minutes.
  • Longer videos are possible but should have a reason: a large project, several unit types or a detailed site.

Pick a target and let the storyboard confirm it. The final duration is agreed in the quote.

4. The route and key moments

List the spaces in the order you want them seen, and mark two or three "moments" that matter most — the view from the terrace, the double-height lounge, the kitchen island. A good walkthrough slows down for those and moves briskly through corridors.

Before production, I draw the camera path over the plan and describe each shot. You approve that storyboard, and the video follows it. Changing the route at this stage costs very little; changing it after rendering costs a lot.

5. Aspect ratio: where will it be watched?

  • 16:9 (horizontal) suits presentations, websites, YouTube and screens at events.
  • 9:16 (vertical) suits Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Stories and WhatsApp Status.

If you need both, say so at the start. Composing a vertical version deliberately gives far better results than cropping a horizontal video afterwards.

6. Resolution

Full HD (1080p) works well for most online use. 4K may be worth it for large screens or events, but it increases rendering time and file sizes. Choose based on where the video will be shown.

7. Branding and text

Decide whether the video should include:

  • An opening or closing title with the project name
  • A logo and contact details
  • Short on-screen captions for rooms or features

Keep text minimal. Many people watch social videos without sound, so short captions can help — but long paragraphs on screen rarely get read.

8. Music and sound

Music sets the pace and mood. It must be properly licensed for your use — commercial use in an advert is not the same as personal use. Options include tracks licensed for the project or music you already have the right to use. Mention any preferences early.

9. Built from your drawings

A walkthrough is produced from a 3D model built from your plans, elevations and sections. The camera moves through consistent geometry, so rooms, openings and proportions follow the drawings from shot to shot. The more complete the drawings, the more faithful the result.

10. What to send

  • Floor plans and elevations (and sections if levels change), or an existing 3D model
  • Material and furniture references for every space that appears
  • The route and key moments
  • Target duration, aspect ratio(s) and resolution
  • Logo, contact details and any on-screen text
  • Music preferences

The general checklist in What to Send Before Ordering an Architectural Visualization applies here too.

How reviews usually work

  1. Storyboard — approve the route and shots.
  2. Draft edit — often at lower quality, to check pacing and content.
  3. Final render and edit — produced after consolidated feedback.

The number of revision rounds is agreed in the quote. Collecting everyone's comments into one list per round keeps the process smooth.

A note on what video can and cannot do

A walkthrough helps people understand a design and imagine being there. It cannot guarantee sales or approvals, and it is not a construction or permit document.
